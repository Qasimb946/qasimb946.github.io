Featured research

Imputing missing data in image time series under realistic cloud masks

Earth-observation image time series have large gaps, and most imputation methods are tested with randomly placed holes. Real gaps are shaped like clouds. I evaluated imputation models on 977 timesteps of MODIS Aqua chlorophyll-a maps (8-day composites at 4 km, Pearl River Estuary, 2003–2023) using realistic cloud-shaped masks, and measured how much the choice of test changes the conclusion.

What I found

  • The test hid the failure. A model trained on random masks looked fine on random-mask tests and got 2.96× worse on cloud-shaped masks.
  • Two changes drove most of the gain. Training with structured, cloud-aware masks and widening the model's spatial reach (K from 1 to 7) each cut error by roughly 11–13%.
  • One popular idea added little. Joint space-time attention contributed only a small improvement.
  • There is a cost. On random masks, the best model is about 10% worse than the published method. I report this because it shows the gains come from matching training to realistic conditions, not from a uniformly better model.
Method and setup
  • Models: transformer and diffusion-based imputation models in PyTorch and TensorFlow; the best model has about 137K parameters.
  • Design: single-variable ablations over 15 model variants across 2 missingness regimes (random masks and cloud-shaped masks).
  • Compute: single GPU per job on a Slurm cluster (MSU Tempest, CUDA, NVIDIA A40/A100), 1000+ GPU-hours across the project.
  • Baseline: the published STIMP training recipe, evaluated on the same held-out data.
  • Hyperparameter search with Bayesian optimization; feature analysis with SHAP.
Limits and open questions
  • The diffusion backbone gives sample-based uncertainty, but I have not measured its calibration. I do not claim the uncertainty estimates are reliable.
  • I model the geometry of the missing regions (cloud-shaped masks), not the physical process that causes missingness.
  • Next question: can a small set of structured tests predict how a model will fail in deployment before it is deployed?
Status: manuscript in preparation for ICML (submission planned January 2027). Results have been approved by the Ph.D. committee.

Research study · Python, PyTorch

I reproduced and trained five architectures (BERT-base, R-GAT, R-GAT+BERT, TextGT, TextGT+BERT) on SemEval-2014 Restaurant and Laptop data and on Twitter data: 15 checkpoints, matching published accuracy within 1–3 points. Then I looked past accuracy.

Nonparametric and tree-based models: a baseline-controlled benchmark

2026 · Python, NumPy, Pandas

I built k-nearest neighbors, condensed k-NN and decision trees with reduced-error pruning from scratch and compared them against null baselines on 6 datasets using 5×2 nested cross-validation.

  • 51% lower MSE than the baseline on Abalone and 77% lower on Computer Hardware.
  • No signal on Forest Fires, reported as such. Pruning cut test MSE by 35–45%.
